# A high win rate is purchasable, and it is not profit

## The question
Can a rule be tuned to win nine times out of ten? And if it can, is that worth having?

## How it was tested
A wide sweep of exit geometries was run over the same entries. Each variant was scored on win
rate and, separately, on money after costs.

## What came back
A very high win rate is easy to buy. Take profit sooner and move the stop further away, and the
win rate climbs as far as you like. The money goes the other way at the same time, because the
rare loss grows faster than the frequent win shrinks.

There is a point where the curve crosses. Past it, every further point of win rate costs more in
the tail than it adds in frequency. A run that reached the high nineties on win rate lost money
over the same trades.

## What to take from it
Win rate is a property of the exit, not evidence of an edge. Score a strategy on money after
costs, and treat an unqualified win rate as marketing. If someone quotes you a win rate without
an average loss beside it, they have told you nothing.
