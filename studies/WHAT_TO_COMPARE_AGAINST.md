# The benchmark question

## The question
A system returned a certain amount over a year. Was that good?

## Why it is harder than it looks
A return has no meaning on its own. It needs three things beside it: the risk taken to get it, the
capital that had to be committed, and what the obvious alternative would have paid.

The obvious alternative is usually buying the index and doing nothing. For a strategy that holds
risk for a fraction of the day, the honest comparison is harder still, because the capital sat
idle the rest of the time and idle capital earns something.

## What was measured
Comparing on return alone ranked variants one way. Comparing on return per unit of capital
actually committed ranked them differently, and comparing on return per unit of drawdown
differently again. The ordering was not stable across the three.

That instability is the finding. A variant that wins on one measure and loses on another has not
been shown to be better. It has been shown to be different.

## What to take from it
Decide the measure before the test, write it down, and report it next to the obvious alternative.
State the capital committed and the worst drawdown in the same breath as the return. A number
quoted without those is not a result.
